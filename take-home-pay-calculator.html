TAKE-HOME PAY

What is take-home pay?

Take-home pay is the amount of money left from your gross earnings after estimated taxes and payroll deductions are removed.

Gross pay

Gross pay is your income before federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, state taxes, and deductions.

Federal income tax

Federal income tax is estimated using your filing status, taxable income, deductions, and applicable 2026 federal tax rules.

FICA taxes

FICA includes Social Security and Medicare taxes that are generally withheld from employee wages.

Payroll deductions

Benefits, retirement contributions, loan repayments, garnishments, and other payroll deductions can change your final take-home pay.

GROSS VS NET

Gross pay vs. take-home pay

Your salary is not the same as the amount that reaches your bank account.

Gross pay

Gross pay is the total amount you earn before payroll taxes and deductions.

Net pay

Net pay is the amount remaining after taxes and deductions. It is commonly called take-home pay.

Why the difference matters

Comparing salaries using gross income alone can be misleading because taxes and deductions vary between workers.

Your paycheck may vary

Actual withholding can change because of Form W-4 elections, benefits, local taxes, year-to-date wages, and employer payroll rules.

FAQ

Take-home pay calculator FAQ

What is take-home pay?

Take-home pay is the amount you receive after estimated federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, state taxes, and payroll deductions are subtracted from gross pay.

How is take-home pay calculated?

Take-home pay starts with gross income. Estimated federal tax, Social Security, Medicare, applicable state taxes, and payroll deductions are then subtracted.

How much is $75,000 after taxes?

The take-home amount from a $75,000 salary depends on your filing status, state, dependents, pay frequency, and deductions. Use the calculator above for a personalized estimate.

What is the difference between gross pay and take-home pay?

Gross pay is your earnings before taxes and deductions. Take-home pay, also called net pay, is what remains after estimated taxes and deductions.

Does pay frequency affect take-home pay?

Pay frequency changes the amount and timing of individual paychecks. Your annual salary generally represents the same yearly gross income, although withholding calculations can vary by pay schedule.
