MONTHLY PAYCHECK GUIDE

What is a monthly paycheck?

A monthly paycheck is a paycheck issued once each month. Under a standard monthly payroll schedule, an employee receives 12 paychecks during the year. Your gross monthly pay is generally based on your annual salary divided by 12, while your actual take-home pay depends on taxes and payroll deductions.

Gross monthly pay

Gross monthly pay is the amount you earn before federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, state taxes, insurance premiums, retirement contributions, and other deductions are taken out.

Monthly take-home pay

Take-home pay is the amount left after estimated taxes and deductions. It is the amount that is generally available to spend, save, or use toward financial goals.

Monthly payroll means 12 checks

If an employer pays monthly throughout a full year, annual salary is normally spread across 12 pay periods. This differs from semimonthly and biweekly payroll.

Your paycheck can vary

Your actual check can differ from a simple salary calculation because withholding depends on your W-4, benefits, deductions, state rules, year-to-date wages, and other payroll details.

MONTHLY PAYCHECK FORMULA

How to calculate monthly pay from annual salary

The first step is converting annual gross income into monthly gross pay. Taxes and deductions are considered separately when estimating take-home pay.

Gross monthly pay formula
Annual salary ÷ 12 = Gross monthly pay

Example: $60,000 salary

$60,000 ÷ 12 = $5,000 gross monthly pay. This is before federal income tax, FICA, state taxes, and other deductions.

Example: $90,000 salary

$90,000 ÷ 12 = $7,500 gross monthly pay. The amount deposited into your account will normally be lower after taxes and deductions.

Important: Gross monthly pay and monthly take-home pay are not the same thing. The calculator estimates what remains after the taxes and deductions entered above.

TAXES AND WITHHOLDING

What comes out of a monthly paycheck?

Several different taxes and deductions can reduce gross monthly earnings before you receive your paycheck.

Federal income tax

Federal income tax withholding depends on earnings, filing status, Form W-4 information, and other withholding adjustments. The IRS explains that withholding is affected by the amount earned during the payroll period and information provided on Form W-4.

Social Security

Social Security is part of FICA. For 2026, the employee Social Security withholding rate is 6.2%, subject to the annual Social Security wage base.

Medicare

Medicare is also part of FICA. The standard employee Medicare tax rate for 2026 is 1.45%, with an additional Medicare tax applying to wages above the applicable threshold.

State income tax

State tax treatment varies by state. Some states do not impose a broad individual income tax, while others apply state income tax. This calculator uses the centralized state rules available in the PaycheckMint tax engine.

PAYROLL DEDUCTIONS

Pre-tax and post-tax deductions

Your paycheck can contain deductions that are separate from income taxes. Understanding the difference can help you explain why gross salary and take-home pay are far apart.

Pre-tax deductions

Depending on the type of benefit and payroll treatment, pre-tax deductions can reduce the amount of income considered for certain taxes.

  • Traditional 401(k) contributions
  • Health insurance premiums
  • HSA contributions
  • FSA contributions
  • Other eligible pre-tax deductions

Post-tax deductions

Post-tax deductions are generally taken after applicable taxes have been calculated. They still reduce the amount ultimately deposited into your account.

  • Roth 401(k) contributions
  • Loan repayments
  • Garnishments
  • Other post-tax payroll deductions

PAY SCHEDULES

Monthly vs. semimonthly vs. biweekly pay

The same annual salary can produce different paycheck amounts depending on the employer's payroll schedule. The annual amount does not automatically change just because the pay frequency changes.

Monthly pay

Normally 12 paychecks per year. Annual salary is divided by 12 to determine gross pay per paycheck.

Semimonthly pay

Normally 24 paychecks per year. Employees are usually paid twice each month, often on predetermined dates.

Biweekly pay

Normally 26 paychecks per year. Employees receive a paycheck every two weeks, which means some months can contain three paychecks.

Weekly pay

Normally 52 paychecks per year. Weekly payroll can make each individual check smaller even though annual gross salary remains the same.

USING YOUR MONTHLY PAYCHECK

How to use monthly take-home pay for budgeting

Your take-home pay is often a more practical starting point for a monthly budget than your gross salary because it reflects the amount available after payroll deductions.

Start with net income

Use estimated monthly take-home pay as the income starting point for your household budget.

Account for fixed expenses

Include recurring expenses such as housing, utilities, insurance, minimum debt payments, and other obligations.

Plan savings separately

Consider emergency savings, retirement contributions, short-term goals, and other financial priorities.

Leave room for variable expenses

Food, transportation, entertainment, and other variable costs can change from month to month, so avoid assigning every dollar to a fixed expense.

FAQ

Monthly paycheck calculator FAQ

How is a monthly paycheck calculated?

For a monthly payroll schedule, annual gross income is generally divided by 12 to determine gross monthly pay. Estimated federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, state taxes, and deductions are then subtracted to estimate take-home pay.

How much is $60,000 a year per month?

A $60,000 annual salary equals $5,000 in gross monthly pay before taxes and deductions.

How much is $75,000 a year per month?

A $75,000 annual salary equals $6,250 in gross monthly pay before taxes and deductions.

How much is $100,000 a year per month?

A $100,000 annual salary equals $8,333.33 in gross monthly pay before taxes and deductions.

Is monthly pay the same as semimonthly pay?

No. Monthly pay normally means 12 paychecks per year. Semimonthly pay normally means 24 paychecks per year, usually two each month.

Is biweekly pay the same as twice-a-month pay?

No. Biweekly pay means a paycheck every two weeks and normally results in 26 paychecks per year. Semimonthly pay normally produces 24 paychecks per year.

Why is my take-home pay lower than my gross monthly pay?

Gross pay is your earnings before deductions. Take-home pay is reduced by applicable federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, state taxes, retirement contributions, insurance, and other payroll deductions.

Does my state affect my monthly paycheck?

Yes. State tax rules can affect take-home pay. The amount can differ substantially depending on where you work and live and how your state treats individual income.

Can deductions change my monthly take-home pay?

Yes. Retirement contributions, insurance, HSA/FSA contributions, loan repayments, garnishments, and other deductions can change the amount deposited into your account.

How accurate is this calculator?

It is an estimate rather than a payroll statement. Actual withholding can differ because of Form W-4 information, employer benefits, local taxes, payroll practices, year-to-date wages, and other circumstances.
