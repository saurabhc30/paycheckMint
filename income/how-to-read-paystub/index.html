PAYCHECK GUIDE

How to Read a Pay Stub

Your pay stub contains much more than the amount deposited into your bank account. Learn how to read each section, understand gross and net pay, identify taxes and deductions, and use year-to-date numbers to check your earnings.

View a sample pay stub → Calculate take-home pay
✓
Quick answer A pay stub shows what you earned, what was withheld or deducted, what has accumulated year to date, and the net amount you actually receive.

The easiest way to read a pay stub is to follow the money from earnings to gross pay, then through taxes and deductions, and finally to net pay.

01

Read your pay stub in 6 steps

Pay stub layouts vary by employer and payroll provider, but the important information generally follows the same basic pattern.

📅

1. Check the pay period

Find the beginning and ending dates for the work period covered by the paycheck.

💼

2. Review earnings

Look for regular wages, salary, overtime, bonuses, commissions or other earnings.

💵

3. Find gross pay

Gross pay is the earnings amount before applicable taxes and employee deductions.

🧾

4. Review taxes

Check federal, state, local and payroll-tax withholdings shown on the statement.

🏦

5. Review deductions

Look for retirement contributions, health benefits and other deductions.

✓

6. Find net pay

Net pay is the amount left after applicable taxes and deductions are subtracted.

03

Check the pay period and pay date

The pay period tells you which dates the paycheck covers. The pay date tells you when your employer issued the payment.

📅

Pay period

This is the period during which you earned the wages shown on the statement.

💳

Pay date

This is the date the paycheck or direct deposit is scheduled to be paid.

!

If you are comparing your paycheck with your work schedule, make sure you compare the correct pay period rather than only looking at the date the money reached your bank account.

04

Understand your earnings

The earnings section explains how your gross wages were calculated for that paycheck.

💼

Regular pay

Your normal wages or salary for the pay period.

⏱

Overtime

Additional earnings for qualifying overtime hours when applicable.

🎁

Bonus

Additional compensation such as a performance bonus or other incentive payment.

📈

Commission

Compensation based on sales or another performance-based arrangement.

05

Find your gross pay

Gross pay is the amount of earnings before applicable taxes and deductions are taken out.

Depending on how you are paid, gross pay may come from salary, hourly wages, overtime, bonuses, commissions or other compensation.

Basic relationship
Gross Pay − Taxes − Deductions = Net Pay
Actual payroll calculations can include different tax and deduction treatments.
06

Identify the taxes withheld

Your pay stub may show several payroll tax withholdings. The exact items depend on your circumstances and location.

🇺🇸

Federal income tax

Federal income tax withholding is generally based on your wages and the withholding information provided to your employer.

🏛

State income tax

Some states impose individual income taxes, while others do not.

🛡

Social Security

Social Security payroll tax is generally shown separately from federal income tax.

🏥

Medicare

Medicare payroll tax is generally listed as a separate payroll withholding.

07

Review deductions and benefits

Not everything taken from your paycheck is an income tax. Your pay stub may also show employee deductions for benefits, retirement plans and other programs.

🏥

Health insurance

Employee contributions toward medical, dental or vision coverage may appear here.

🏦

Retirement

Contributions to a workplace retirement plan may be listed separately from taxes.

🩺

HSA or FSA

Eligible health spending contributions can appear as separate payroll deductions.

📋

Other deductions

Depending on your employer, there may be additional deductions or employee benefits.

08

Understand current vs. YTD amounts

One of the most useful parts of a pay stub is the difference between current and YTD amounts.

YTD means year to date. It generally represents the cumulative amounts recorded from the beginning of the calendar or payroll year through the current paycheck.

09

Find your net pay

Net pay is the amount that remains after applicable taxes and employee deductions have been taken from your earnings.

This is usually the number most closely connected to the amount you actually receive through direct deposit or another payment method.

11

What should you check on every pay stub?

Reading your pay stub isn't only about understanding the numbers. It can also help you spot unexpected changes.

✓
Confirm the pay period and pay date.
✓
Compare your regular hours or salary with the earnings shown.
✓
Check overtime, bonuses or commissions when applicable.
✓
Review new or changed taxes and deductions.
✓
Compare current and YTD totals for unexpected differences.
✓
Make sure your net pay matches the amount you expected to receive.
12

Pre-tax vs. post-tax deductions

Some deductions can receive different tax treatment. Your pay stub may identify whether a deduction is taken before or after certain taxes are calculated.

↓

Pre-tax deduction

A qualifying deduction may reduce the amount of wages subject to certain taxes, depending on the type of benefit and applicable rules.

→

Post-tax deduction

A post-tax deduction is generally taken after the relevant taxes have been calculated.

!

Tax treatment can vary by deduction and tax type. Don't assume that every deduction labeled "pre-tax" affects every payroll tax in the same way.

13

Protect your pay stub

Pay stubs can contain sensitive personal and employment information. Avoid posting an unedited pay stub publicly or sending it to people who do not need it.

!
Remove your full name and employee identification information when sharing a sample online.
!
Avoid exposing bank account or direct-deposit information.
!
Keep digital copies stored somewhere secure.
