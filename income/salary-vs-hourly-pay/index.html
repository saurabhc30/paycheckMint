Salary vs Hourly Pay: What's the Difference?

Salary and hourly pay can produce very different paychecks even when the advertised annual amounts look similar. Learn how to compare the two, including overtime, work hours, benefits, taxes and total compensation.

Salary vs hourly pay at a glance

The main difference is how your regular pay is calculated.

An hourly employee is generally paid based on the number of hours worked and the applicable hourly rate. A salaried employee is generally paid a predetermined amount for a pay period based on an annual salary.

Neither number tells the entire story. When comparing jobs, you should also consider expected weekly hours, overtime, paid time off, health insurance, retirement benefits, bonuses and other compensation.

How hourly pay works

With hourly pay, your gross earnings are generally tied to the number of hours you work and your hourly rate.

Regular Pay = Hourly Rate × Hours Worked

For example, someone earning $24 per hour who works 40 hours in a week would have regular gross earnings of $960 for that week before taxes and deductions.

How salary pay works

A salary is generally stated as an annual amount. Your employer then divides that amount among the pay periods used by the company.

Approximate Regular Paycheck = Annual Salary ÷ Number of Paychecks

For example, a $60,000 annual salary paid twice a month would produce 24 regular gross paychecks of approximately $2,500 each before taxes and deductions.

Your actual paycheck can be different because of taxes, insurance, retirement contributions, other deductions, bonuses or adjustments.

How to convert salary to hourly pay

A common way to estimate an hourly equivalent is to divide annual salary by estimated annual working hours.

Hourly Equivalent = Annual Salary ÷ Annual Hours Worked

A traditional full-time estimate uses 40 hours per week for 52 weeks:

40 × 52 = 2,080 hours
Example: $65,000 salary
Annual salary $65,000
Estimated hourly equivalent $65,000 ÷ 2,080 ≈ $31.25/hour

This is a simple comparison, not necessarily the employee's legal hourly rate or overtime calculation. If the job requires substantially more or fewer hours, the effective hourly value changes.

How to convert hourly pay to annual salary

You can estimate annual earnings from an hourly rate by multiplying the rate by expected weekly hours and the number of weeks worked.

Annual Earnings = Hourly Rate × Weekly Hours × Weeks Worked

Using 40 hours per week and 52 weeks:

$30 × 40 × 52 = $62,400

Therefore, $30 per hour produces approximately $62,400 in gross annual earnings if someone works 40 hours every week for 52 weeks.

Overtime can change the comparison

One of the most important things to examine when comparing salary and hourly jobs is overtime. The fact that a worker receives a salary does not, by itself, answer whether the worker is entitled to overtime.

Overtime eligibility depends on applicable wage-and-hour requirements and factors such as the employee's duties, compensation and exemption status.

Why expected hours matter

Consider two jobs that both advertise approximately $62,400 per year. If one requires 40 hours per week and the other regularly requires 50 hours, their effective hourly values are different.

$62,400 40 $30.00
$62,400 45 $26.67
$62,400 50 $24.00

*Simple annual-pay divided by estimated annual hours. This table does not determine overtime eligibility or overtime compensation.

Does salary or hourly pay affect taxes?

Salary versus hourly status does not automatically mean that one worker pays a higher tax rate. Taxes generally depend on taxable income, filing circumstances, deductions, withholding elections and applicable federal, state and local rules.

What can change is the amount of gross income you earn. For example, an hourly employee who works additional qualifying hours may earn more in a particular year than someone with a fixed salary.

Your paycheck withholding is also different from your final tax liability. Payroll withholding is an amount withheld during the year; your actual tax liability is determined when your tax return is calculated.

How to compare a salary job with an hourly job

When comparing two offers, convert both into a common framework instead of looking at the headline number alone.

  • Write down the guaranteed annual base pay.
  • Estimate the actual hours you expect to work each week.
  • Check whether overtime may apply and how it is handled.
  • Compare health insurance costs and employer contributions.
  • Compare retirement contributions or matching.
  • Compare paid vacation, holidays and sick leave.
  • Include guaranteed bonuses and clearly identify variable compensation.
  • Consider commute, schedule, remote-work arrangements and other job-specific costs.

Common mistakes when comparing salary and hourly pay

1. Comparing annual salary with hourly rate directly

A number such as $30/hour needs an assumed number of hours and weeks before it can be compared with an annual salary.

2. Ignoring expected work hours

A higher annual salary can correspond to a lower effective hourly value if the job consistently requires substantially more hours.

3. Assuming every salaried employee is exempt

Salary status and overtime exemption are not interchangeable concepts. Review the actual classification and applicable rules.

4. Ignoring benefits

Two jobs with similar base pay can have different total compensation because their benefit packages differ.

5. Comparing gross pay instead of take-home pay

Taxes, retirement contributions, insurance premiums and other deductions can change the amount that reaches your bank account.
