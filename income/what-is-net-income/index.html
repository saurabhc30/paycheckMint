What Is Net Income?

Net income is the amount remaining after applicable taxes, deductions, or business expenses are accounted for. For U.S. employees, the related paycheck concept is usually called net pay or take-home pay.

Quick Answer

In a personal paycheck context, net income can refer to money left after applicable taxes and deductions. In business accounting, net income generally means profit remaining after expenses and taxes. Because the term can have different meanings, the context matters.

What Does Net Income Mean?

The meaning of net income depends on how the term is being used. For an individual employee, people may use net income to describe the money left after taxes and deductions. On a paycheck, the more precise term for the amount actually received is net pay or take-home pay.

In business and investing, net income generally means the profit a company has left after its expenses and applicable taxes have been deducted from revenue.

Paycheck tip: If you want to know how much money will actually reach your bank account, look at your paycheck's net pay rather than simply your annual salary or gross pay.

What Is the Net Income Formula?

For a simple employee paycheck explanation, you can think of net pay as gross pay minus applicable taxes and deductions.

Net Pay = Gross Pay − Taxes − Deductions
Actual payroll calculations can be more detailed and depend on the type of tax and deduction.

This simple formula helps explain why your salary or hourly earnings are usually higher than the amount deposited into your bank account.

Gross Income vs. Net Income

The difference between gross and net income is mainly what has been deducted or accounted for. Gross income generally represents an amount before applicable deductions or adjustments, while net income represents an amount remaining afterward.

Term Meaning Paycheck example
Gross pay Earnings before payroll deductions. Salary or hourly earnings before withholding.
Gross income Income before applicable deductions or adjustments, depending on context. Starting income amount.
Net pay Amount remaining after applicable paycheck deductions. Amount actually received from the paycheck.
Net income Meaning varies by context; can refer to personal income after deductions or business profit after expenses. Depends on how the term is being used.
Take-home pay Common term for money received after paycheck deductions. Your paycheck amount after deductions.

Net Pay vs. Net Income

Net pay and net income are sometimes used interchangeably in everyday conversations, but they are not always identical terms.

When discussing an employee's paycheck, net pay is the clearest term for the amount the employee receives after applicable payroll deductions. Net income can have a broader meaning, particularly in personal finance and business accounting.

For a company, net income normally refers to profit after revenue, expenses, and applicable taxes have been accounted for.

How Net Income Works on a U.S. Paycheck

Your U.S. paycheck can contain several types of withholding and deductions. These reduce the amount available as take-home pay.

Federal income tax Federal income tax withholding can be taken from employee wages.
Social Security A federal payroll tax generally withheld from covered employee wages.
Medicare A federal payroll tax generally withheld from covered employee wages.
State tax State income tax may apply depending on the employee's state and circumstances.
Local tax Some locations impose local income taxes or other payroll-related taxes.
Other deductions Insurance, retirement contributions and other employer-plan deductions may reduce take-home pay.

Net Income Example: $75,000 Salary

Suppose an employee earns a $75,000 annual salary. Their gross annual salary is $75,000, but their actual paycheck will normally be lower after applicable withholding and deductions.

Illustrative monthly paycheck
Annual salary $75,000
Gross monthly pay $6,250
Federal income tax − $700
Social Security & Medicare − $478
Other taxes/deductions − $250
Illustrative net pay $4,822

This is an illustrative example, not a tax calculation for every employee. Actual take-home pay depends on factors such as filing status, Form W-4 information, state and local taxes, benefits, retirement contributions, and other deductions.

What Deductions Reduce Net Pay?

Several types of deductions can reduce the amount you receive from a paycheck. Not every deduction applies to every employee.

  • Federal income tax withholding
  • Social Security tax
  • Medicare tax
  • State income tax, where applicable
  • Local income tax, where applicable
  • Health insurance premiums
  • Dental and vision insurance
  • Retirement plan contributions
  • HSA or FSA contributions, when applicable
  • Other voluntary or required deductions

Pre-Tax vs. Post-Tax Deductions

Paycheck deductions can also be classified according to when they are applied for tax purposes.

Pre-tax deductions

Certain eligible deductions can be taken into account before particular taxes are calculated. The tax treatment depends on the specific benefit, plan and applicable rules.

Post-tax deductions

Post-tax deductions are generally taken after the applicable taxes have been calculated. Their treatment depends on the particular deduction.

Do not assume that every benefit or deduction is pre-tax for every type of tax. Payroll tax treatment can vary by deduction and plan.

Net Income vs. Taxable Income

Net income and taxable income are not interchangeable terms.

Taxable income is a tax concept determined under applicable tax rules. It should not be confused with the amount that reaches your bank account after payroll deductions.

Your paycheck's net pay can therefore be different from your taxable income.

How to Find Net Pay on Your Pay Stub

If you want to know your actual paycheck net pay, look for a line labeled Net Pay, Net Earnings, or a similar term on your pay stub.

Your pay stub may also show the following information:

  • Current gross pay
  • Federal income tax
  • Social Security
  • Medicare
  • State and local taxes
  • Benefit deductions
  • Retirement contributions
  • Year-to-date totals
  • Current net pay

Be careful not to confuse the current pay-period amount with your year-to-date totals.

Why Is My Net Pay Different From My Salary?

Your salary is generally a gross amount. Your paycheck is reduced by applicable taxes and deductions before you receive your take-home pay.

Your net pay can also change from one paycheck to another. Common reasons include:

  • A change in federal tax withholding
  • A change to Form W-4 information
  • Overtime or supplemental wages
  • A bonus or commission
  • Changes in insurance deductions
  • Changes in retirement contributions
  • State or local tax differences
  • One-time payroll deductions

How Pay Frequency Affects Your Paycheck

Your annual salary does not change simply because you are paid weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly or monthly, but the amount on each paycheck does.

Pay frequency Typical number of paychecks Simple salary division
Weekly 52 Annual salary ÷ 52
Biweekly 26 Annual salary ÷ 26
Semi-monthly 24 Annual salary ÷ 24
Monthly 12 Annual salary ÷ 12

These divisions show gross pay before deductions. Actual net pay requires the applicable payroll taxes and deductions to be considered.

How to Calculate Net Income From a Paycheck

  1. Start with your gross pay for the pay period.
  2. Determine applicable federal income tax withholding.
  3. Account for Social Security and Medicare taxes.
  4. Account for state and local taxes where applicable.
  5. Subtract applicable pre-tax and post-tax deductions.
  6. The remaining paycheck amount is your estimated take-home pay or net pay.

The exact calculation can be more complicated because tax withholding and deductions depend on individual circumstances.

Frequently Asked Questions About Net Income

What is net income?

Net income can have different meanings depending on context. For an employee, it can refer to income remaining after applicable taxes and deductions. In business accounting, net income generally refers to profit remaining after expenses and taxes.

What is the net income formula?

For a simple paycheck estimate, net pay can be thought of as gross pay minus applicable taxes and deductions. Actual payroll calculations depend on the employee's circumstances and the type of deduction.

Is net income the same as take-home pay?

In everyday paycheck conversations, net income may be used to describe money remaining after deductions. Net pay or take-home pay is the more specific term for the amount an employee actually receives.

What is the difference between gross income and net income?

Gross income generally refers to income before applicable deductions or adjustments, while net income refers to an amount remaining after applicable deductions, taxes or expenses depending on the context.

What deductions reduce net pay?

Common paycheck deductions can include federal income tax withholding, Social Security, Medicare, state or local taxes where applicable, health insurance, retirement contributions and other deductions.

Is net income the same as taxable income?

No. Net income and taxable income are different concepts. Taxable income is determined under applicable tax rules and is not simply the same thing as take-home pay.

How do I calculate net income from salary?

Start with your gross salary or gross paycheck amount, then account for applicable taxes and deductions. Your actual take-home pay depends on factors such as filing status, withholding, location, benefits and retirement contributions.

Why is my net pay lower than my gross pay?

Net pay is normally lower because taxes and other applicable deductions are taken from gross pay before you receive your paycheck.
