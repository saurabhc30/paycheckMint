The short answer

Gross pay is what you earn before payroll deductions. Net pay is what remains after applicable taxes and other deductions.

The difference in one glance

Think of your paycheck as a journey. Your earnings start as gross pay. Payroll taxes and other deductions are then taken out, leaving the amount you actually receive: net pay.

↑

Gross Pay

The total earnings you receive before applicable taxes and other payroll deductions.

↓

Net Pay

The amount left after applicable taxes and deductions are withheld from your earnings.

Where does the difference go?

The gap between gross pay and net pay can include federal income tax withholding, Social Security and Medicare taxes, state or local taxes where applicable, and other payroll deductions.

What counts as gross pay?

Gross pay generally represents the earnings attributed to your work before applicable payroll deductions. Depending on how you're paid, that can include more than your normal hourly or salary amount.

What is deducted before you receive your pay?

Your employer may withhold several types of taxes and deductions from your paycheck. The exact combination depends on your circumstances and payroll elections.

%
Federal income tax

Federal withholding is influenced by your earnings and Form W-4 information.

🛡
Social Security

Employee payroll tax withheld from applicable wages.

+
Other deductions

Benefits, retirement contributions and other authorized deductions can also affect net pay.

How is net pay calculated?

The basic relationship is straightforward:

Basic paycheck formula
Gross Pay − Taxes − Deductions = Net Pay

Actual payroll calculations can be more complicated because different taxes and deductions can apply to different types of compensation.

Why two people can have the same gross pay but different net pay

Gross pay alone doesn't tell you exactly how much will reach your bank account.

Two employees earning the same amount can have different net pay because their withholding and payroll deductions may not be identical.

1
Tax withholding

Form W-4 information can affect federal income tax withholding.

2
Benefits

Health insurance and other employee benefits can change deductions.

3
Retirement

Contributions to workplace retirement plans can affect current take-home pay.

Which number should you use for your budget?

For everyday budgeting, net pay is usually the more useful number because it represents the amount available after payroll deductions.

Gross pay is still important when comparing salaries, evaluating compensation and understanding your total earnings.

Frequently asked questions

What is gross pay?

Gross pay is the amount you earn before applicable taxes and other payroll deductions are taken out.

What is net pay?

Net pay is the amount remaining after applicable payroll taxes and other deductions have been withheld.

Is net pay the same as take-home pay?

The terms net pay and take-home pay are commonly used to describe the amount an employee receives after applicable payroll deductions.

Why is my net pay lower than my gross pay?

Payroll taxes and other deductions are generally taken from gross earnings before the remaining amount is paid to you.

Can two people with the same gross pay have different net pay?

Yes. Tax withholding, benefits, retirement contributions, state or local taxes and other deductions can differ between employees.

Is gross pay the same as taxable income?

Not necessarily. Payroll and tax rules determine which types of compensation are subject to particular taxes.
