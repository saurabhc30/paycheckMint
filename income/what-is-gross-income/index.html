What Is Gross Income?

Gross income is one of the most important numbers on a paycheck, job offer, tax document, or income statement. Here's what it means, how to calculate it, and how it differs from the money you actually take home.

Quick answer: Gross income is generally the total income earned before taxes and other deductions are taken out. For employees, gross pay can include regular wages or salary, overtime, bonuses, and other compensation.

Gross income meaning

Gross income is generally the amount of income you earn before taxes, employee deductions, and other withholdings are subtracted.

If you look at a typical employee paycheck, gross pay is usually near the top of the earnings section. Your federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, state taxes, health insurance, retirement contributions, and other deductions may then reduce that amount before you receive your final paycheck.

In simple terms, gross income tells you how much you earned before deductions. Net pay tells you how much remains after applicable deductions.

Example: If your gross pay is $2,500 for a paycheck and $500 is removed for taxes and other deductions, your take-home pay would be $2,000.

Gross income formula

The exact calculation depends on how you earn money. For an employee, gross pay can generally be built from regular earnings plus additional compensation such as overtime, bonuses, commissions, or other eligible earnings.

Basic employee formula
Gross Pay = Regular Earnings + Additional Earnings

For an hourly employee, regular earnings can generally be calculated by multiplying the hourly rate by the number of regular hours worked. Overtime or other earnings are then added when applicable.

Hourly worker
Regular Pay = Hourly Rate × Hours Worked

For a salaried employee, gross annual income is generally the stated salary before deductions. Dividing the annual salary by the number of pay periods can give an estimate of gross pay per paycheck.

What is included in gross income?

What counts as gross income depends on the type of income and the context in which the term is being used. For an employee, gross earnings can include several types of compensation.

💼

Regular wages or salary

Your normal hourly wages or salary generally form the starting point for calculating gross pay.

⏱️

Overtime pay

Overtime compensation generally adds to your gross earnings for the pay period.

🎁

Bonuses

Bonuses and certain other incentive payments can increase gross earnings.

📈

Commissions

Commission-based earnings can contribute to gross compensation when paid.

Gross income vs net income

Gross income and net income are related, but they are not the same number. Confusing the two can make it harder to create a realistic budget or compare job offers.

Gross pay vs gross income

The terms gross pay and gross income are sometimes used interchangeably when discussing an employee's paycheck, but the exact meaning depends on the context.

Gross pay usually refers specifically to compensation from employment for a particular paycheck or period. Gross income can be broader and may refer to income from multiple sources.

For example, an employee might have $5,000 of gross wages during a month and also receive other forms of income. When reviewing a tax or financial situation, the broader concept of gross income may include income beyond a single paycheck.

Gross income examples

Example 1: Hourly employee

Suppose you earn $25 per hour and work 40 regular hours in a week.

Weekly gross pay
$25 × 40 = $1,000

Your gross weekly pay would be $1,000 before applicable taxes and other deductions. Your actual take-home pay would normally be lower.

Example 2: Salaried employee

Suppose your annual salary is $72,000 and you are paid monthly.

Monthly gross pay
$72,000 ÷ 12 = $6,000

Your estimated monthly gross pay would be $6,000 before taxes, insurance, retirement contributions, and other deductions.

Example 3: Hourly employee with overtime

Suppose you earn $20 per hour, work 40 regular hours, and receive 5 hours of overtime paid at 1.5 times your regular hourly rate.

Regular earnings
$20 × 40 = $800
Overtime rate
$20 × 1.5 = $30/hour
Total gross pay
$800 + ($30 × 5) = $950

In this simplified example, gross pay for the week would be $950 before applicable deductions.

Why gross income matters

Gross income is useful for more than reading a paycheck. It can help you understand your earnings, compare job offers, calculate annual compensation, and evaluate how much income you generate before deductions.

1

Comparing job offers

Gross salary or wages provide a starting point when comparing compensation between jobs.

2

Understanding your paycheck

Gross pay helps explain why your paycheck is larger before deductions than the amount deposited into your account.

3

Planning your finances

Gross income provides a useful starting point for understanding earnings before taxes and deductions.

4

Estimating annual earnings

Weekly, biweekly, semimonthly, or monthly gross earnings can be converted into an annual estimate.

Gross income and your paycheck

Your paycheck normally starts with earnings. Those earnings may include regular wages, salary, overtime, commissions, bonuses, or other compensation.

Deductions and withholding can then reduce the amount you actually receive.

Simplified paycheck relationship
Gross Pay − Taxes − Deductions = Take-Home Pay

This is a simplified explanation. Actual payroll calculations can include multiple tax types, pre-tax deductions, post-tax deductions, benefits, and other adjustments.

Where can you find gross income?

If you are trying to find your gross income, several common documents can help.

  • Pay stub: Look for labels such as gross pay, gross wages, current earnings, or year-to-date gross pay.
  • Form W-2: Your annual wage information is reported on your W-2.
  • Job offer: A salary offer usually describes compensation before payroll deductions.
  • Payroll records: Employer payroll systems generally track gross earnings for each pay period.

Gross income vs gross adjusted income

Gross income and adjusted gross income are not interchangeable terms in federal tax discussions.

Gross income can refer broadly to income before certain adjustments. Adjusted gross income, commonly called AGI, is a specific tax concept calculated after certain permitted adjustments.

This distinction matters because some tax calculations, deductions, credits, and eligibility rules use AGI or modified adjusted gross income rather than simply looking at your paycheck's gross pay.

Remember: Your gross paycheck amount is not necessarily the same thing as your federal taxable income, adjusted gross income, or take-home pay. These terms describe different stages or concepts.

Common gross income mistakes

  • Confusing gross pay with take-home pay: Your gross earnings are normally higher than the amount deposited into your account.
  • Ignoring overtime: Additional overtime earnings can increase gross pay for the period.
  • Using one paycheck as annual income: Pay frequency matters when converting periodic earnings into an annual amount.
  • Ignoring bonuses or commissions: Additional compensation can change your gross earnings.
  • Confusing gross income with AGI: These are different concepts used for different purposes.

How to calculate annual gross income

If you are paid a fixed salary, your annual gross income can often be identified directly from your salary amount.

If you are paid hourly, you can estimate annual gross income by multiplying your hourly rate by expected hours worked and the number of working weeks.

Simplified hourly estimate
Hourly Rate × Hours per Week × Weeks per Year

For example, a worker earning $30 per hour for 40 hours per week for 52 weeks would have a simplified annual gross earnings estimate of $62,400 before overtime, unpaid time, bonuses, or deductions.

Gross income and budgeting

Gross income is useful for understanding your overall earnings, but your budget should generally focus on the money you actually have available after taxes and deductions.

If your gross monthly pay is $6,000 but your normal take-home pay is $4,600, building a spending plan around $6,000 could overstate the amount available for everyday expenses.

That's why understanding both gross pay and net pay is important when creating a realistic household budget.

Frequently asked questions

What is gross income?

Gross income is generally the total income earned before taxes and other deductions are taken out. For employees, it can include wages, salary, overtime, bonuses, commissions, and other compensation.

Is gross income before or after taxes?

Gross income is generally measured before taxes and other deductions. The amount remaining after applicable deductions is generally referred to as net pay or take-home pay in a paycheck context.

What is the difference between gross income and net income?

Gross income is generally income before applicable taxes and deductions, while net income refers to what remains after applicable deductions and expenses, depending on the context.

Does gross income include overtime?

Overtime earnings generally contribute to an employee's gross wages for the pay period.

Does gross income include bonuses?

Bonuses can generally increase gross compensation when they are paid. How they are taxed and reported can depend on the circumstances.

Is gross salary the same as gross income?

Not always. Gross salary usually describes employment compensation before deductions, while gross income can be a broader concept that includes income from different sources.
