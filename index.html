HOW IT WORKS

How PaycheckMint estimates your paycheck

PaycheckMint starts with your gross wages and estimates the major taxes and deductions that can reduce your take-home pay.

1. Gross pay

Your salary or hourly earnings are converted into an annual amount and then into your selected pay period.

2. Federal tax

The calculator estimates federal withholding using 2026 federal withholding concepts and your W-4 inputs.

3. FICA

Social Security and Medicare taxes are calculated separately because they follow different rules.

4. State tax

State income-tax estimates vary by state. Local taxes and employer-specific withholding rules may cause your actual paycheck to differ.

2026 TAX INFORMATION

What can affect your 2026 paycheck?

Social Security 6.2% employee tax up to the 2026 wage base.
Medicare 1.45% employee tax with no Social Security-style wage cap.
Additional Medicare 0.9% withholding on wages above $200,000 paid by an employer.
W-4 information Filing status, multiple jobs, credits, other income, deductions and extra withholding can change federal withholding.

METHODOLOGY

Gross pay vs. take-home pay

Gross pay is the amount you earn before taxes and deductions. Take-home pay is the amount remaining after estimated taxes and deductions.

For example, a $60,000 annual salary does not mean you receive $5,000 in your bank account every month. Federal taxes, Social Security, Medicare, state taxes and deductions can reduce the amount of each paycheck.

PaycheckMint is designed to give you a quick planning estimate before you receive your actual payroll statement.

FAQ

Paycheck calculator questions

Is PaycheckMint an exact paycheck calculator?

No. PaycheckMint provides an estimate for planning purposes. Actual payroll withholding can differ based on your employer's payroll system, W-4 information, state rules, local taxes, year-to-date wages and benefit treatment.

How is take-home pay calculated?

The calculator starts with gross wages and estimates federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, state tax and the deductions you enter. The remaining amount is your estimated take-home pay.

Does PaycheckMint calculate overtime?

Yes. Hourly workers can enter regular hours and overtime hours. The calculator estimates overtime at 1.5 times the regular hourly rate.

Why can my real paycheck be different?

Your actual paycheck can differ because employers use detailed federal and state withholding methods. Local taxes, benefit plans, year-to-date wages and your completed Form W-4 can also affect withholding.
