Understanding your take-home pay

Your take-home pay (or net pay) is the amount left in your paycheck after all mandatory tax withholdings and voluntary deductions are subtracted from your total earnings (gross pay). Understanding how gross pay is transformed into net pay helps you build more accurate personal budgets and make informed financial decisions.

This take-home pay calculator evaluates your gross income, tax filing status, state location, and benefits contributions to provide an accurate estimate of your net income per pay period.

Take-Home Pay = Gross Pay - Mandatory Taxes - Payroll Deductions

Mandatory tax withholdings explained

Several mandatory taxes are automatically withheld from your paycheck before it hits your bank account:

  • Federal Income Tax: Calculated based on your IRS Form W-4 elections, tax brackets, and standard deduction allowances.
  • FICA Taxes: Federal Insurance Contributions Act taxes consist of Social Security (6.2%) and Medicare (1.45%), totaling 7.65% for standard employee contributions.
  • State Income Tax: Varies depending on where you reside or work, ranging from 0% in states like Texas and Florida to progressive tax brackets in states like California and New York.
  • Local Taxes: Some municipalities and counties levy additional local income or wage taxes.

Pre-tax vs. post-tax deductions

Deduction Type Tax Impact Common Examples
Pre-Tax Deductions Reduces gross income before taxes are computed, lowering total tax liability. Traditional 401(k), HSA, FSA, health and dental insurance premiums.
Post-Tax Deductions Deducted after all federal, state, and payroll taxes have been calculated. Roth 401(k), wage garnishments, union dues, employee loan repayments.

2026 federal tax assumptions

For 2026, the standard deduction amounts are $16,100 for single filers, $24,150 for heads of household, and $32,200 for married couples filing jointly. The federal tax framework uses seven marginal brackets (10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37%).

Because actual employer payroll processes use specific IRS withholding formulas based on Form W-4 details, individual paycheck stubs may vary slightly from generalized estimates.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between gross pay and take-home pay?

Gross pay is the total amount of money you earn before any deductions or taxes are taken out. Take-home pay (net pay) is the actual amount credited to your bank account after subtracting taxes and deductions.

How do pre-tax deductions increase take-home pay value?

Pre-tax deductions lower your taxable gross income. While they reduce total net cash, they allow you to pay less in income taxes while funding retirement, healthcare, or medical savings accounts.

Why does my pay frequency change my paycheck amount?

Your total annual net pay stays roughly similar, but changing pay frequency divides your earnings across a different number of checks per year (e.g., 52 weekly, 26 biweekly, 24 semimonthly, or 12 monthly checks).

Why does my paycheck differ from the calculation?

Discrepancies usually happen due to specific Form W-4 options, localized tax districts, employer-specific benefit tiers, or mid-year wage threshold caps on Social Security tax.
