What does personal finance include?

Personal finance is the process of managing the money you earn, spend, save, borrow, protect, and invest.

The right approach depends on your income, expenses, goals, household, debt, time horizon, and other circumstances. There is no single budget or financial strategy that works for everyone.

  • ✓ Know how much income you actually have available to spend.
  • ✓ Give regular and irregular expenses a place in your budget.
  • ✓ Keep emergency savings separate from long-term investments.
  • ✓ Understand the cost of borrowing before taking on new debt.
  • ✓ Consider taxes, insurance, and investment risk when planning ahead.

Use calculators as a starting point

A calculator can help you estimate a number, but the result is only part of the decision. Review the assumptions, understand which inputs matter, and use authoritative sources when making important financial or tax decisions.

Personal finance questions

Answers to common questions about organizing your finances.

What are the main areas of personal finance?

Five major areas are taxes, budgeting, credit and debt, investing, and insurance. They cover different parts of managing your money, from understanding your paycheck to protecting assets and planning for long-term goals.

How should I start managing my money?

Start by understanding your income and recurring expenses. Then create a workable budget, build savings for emergencies, manage debt, and develop long-term goals. The order and pace can vary depending on your individual circumstances.

Why is budgeting important?

A budget gives your income a plan before you spend it. It can help you identify spending patterns, prepare for upcoming expenses, and direct money toward savings, debt repayment, or other goals.

When should I start investing?

Investing is generally intended for longer-term goals. Before investing, consider your near-term cash needs, emergency savings, existing debt, time horizon, and ability to tolerate losses.

What is the difference between saving and investing?

Saving generally focuses on keeping money available for near-term needs, while investing involves buying assets with the expectation of long-term growth. Investments can lose value, so money needed in the near term may need a different approach.

Financial information sources

For official information about federal taxes, consumer credit, investing, and insurance, consult government and regulatory sources such as the Internal Revenue Service, Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, Investor.gov, and National Association of Insurance Commissioners.
