Long-Term Care Insurance: How It Works, Costs & Coverage

Long-term care insurance can help pay for extended care when you need help with everyday activities or ongoing supportive services. Learn what it covers, how benefits work, what Medicare does and does not cover, and the policy features worth reviewing.

Quick answer

Long-term care insurance helps pay for qualifying long-term care services. Depending on the policy, that can include care at home, assisted living, adult day care or a nursing facility. Long-term care is different from ordinary medical care: much of it involves help with everyday activities and ongoing support. Medicare generally does not pay for long-term custodial care, so people may use personal savings, insurance or qualifying Medicaid assistance to pay for care.

01

Care can happen at home

Long-term care isn't limited to nursing homes. Policies may cover qualifying home and community-based services.

02

Medicare is different

Medicare generally does not cover ongoing long-term custodial care.

03

Policy details matter

Benefit amounts, elimination periods, inflation protection and covered services can vary significantly.

What is long-term care?

Long-term care is ongoing assistance for people who have difficulty caring for themselves because of illness, disability, aging or another qualifying condition.

It can involve help with everyday activities such as bathing, dressing, eating and using the bathroom. It can also include other supportive services provided at home, in an assisted living facility, an adult day care setting or a nursing facility.

Long-term care is therefore not the same thing as a hospital stay or a short course of medical treatment.

Think of long-term care as ongoing support rather than a single medical event. A person may need care for an extended period even when the primary need is assistance with daily activities rather than treatment intended to cure an illness.

What does long-term care insurance cover?

Coverage depends on the policy, but long-term care insurance may help pay for several different types of qualifying care.

1

Home care

A policy may help pay for covered services provided in your own home, subject to the policy's requirements and limits.

2

Assisted living

Some policies provide benefits for qualifying care in assisted living facilities.

3

Nursing facility care

Depending on the policy, benefits may help pay for qualifying skilled, intermediate or custodial care in a nursing facility.

4

Adult day care

Some policies include covered services in adult day care or community-based settings.

5

Respite and other services

Depending on the policy, covered benefits can include respite care, hospice-related services and other qualifying long-term care services.

Long-term care insurance vs. health insurance

Health insurance and long-term care insurance serve different purposes. Health insurance is primarily designed around medical treatment, while long-term care insurance can help pay for ongoing assistance and supportive care.

Feature Health insurance Long-term care insurance
Main purpose Medical treatment and healthcare services. Qualifying long-term supportive and custodial care.
Home services May cover certain medically necessary home health services. May cover qualifying long-term home care under the policy.
Nursing facility Certain skilled care may be covered under specific circumstances. May cover qualifying long-term nursing facility care.
Daily living assistance Generally not designed to pay for ongoing custodial assistance. Often an important purpose of long-term care coverage.

Does Medicare cover long-term care?

Medicare generally does not pay for long-term custodial care. Medicare explains that long-term care includes medical and non-medical services for people with chronic illness or disability, including help with activities of daily living. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

Medicare can cover certain skilled nursing facility services and other healthcare services under specific circumstances. That is different from paying for ongoing custodial long-term care.

This distinction is important when estimating retirement and healthcare costs. Having Medicare does not necessarily mean that all future long-term care expenses will be paid by Medicare.

Medicare vs. long-term care

If you are planning for future care, separate your expected medical expenses from potential long-term care expenses. They may be paid through different sources.

How long-term care insurance benefits work

Long-term care policies can structure benefits in different ways. A policy may specify a daily or monthly maximum benefit and a maximum benefit period or pool of benefits.

For example, a policy could provide a stated maximum daily benefit that can be used for covered services. The actual amount available can depend on the type of care and the policy's reimbursement rules.

Some policies use a maximum lifetime benefit pool rather than simply promising a fixed dollar amount for an unlimited period.

What is an elimination period?

The elimination period is the period that must be satisfied before long-term care insurance benefits begin.

NAIC consumer guidance notes that policies can use different elimination periods, such as 20, 30, 60, 90 or 100 days. The policy also determines how the days are counted. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

A longer elimination period can reduce premiums in some policies, but it also means you may have to pay more of the initial care costs yourself.

Read how the waiting period is calculated

Don't look only at the number of days. Check whether the policy uses calendar days or service days and whether a new elimination period can apply after another episode of care.

Why inflation protection matters

Long-term care may be needed many years after a policy is purchased. A benefit that appears substantial today may cover a smaller share of care costs in the future.

Inflation protection is designed to increase the policy's benefit amounts over time. NAIC notes that automatic inflation protection can increase benefits annually, often by a specified percentage. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

The exact feature matters. Check whether increases are simple or compounded, how long they continue and how they affect premiums.

How much does long-term care insurance cost?

There is no single price for long-term care insurance. Premiums can vary based on factors such as age, health, coverage amount, benefit period, elimination period, inflation protection and the policy's features.

Your location and the insurance company can also matter. NAIC recommends comparing benefits, covered facilities, policy limits, premiums and the insurer's history of rate increases when shopping for coverage. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

A lower premium does not necessarily mean lower total financial risk. Compare what you actually receive for the premium.

Can long-term care insurance premiums increase?

Premium increases can be an important issue with long-term care insurance. NAIC notes that the market has experienced rate increases, particularly among older policy generations, as insurers' assumptions about claims and policyholder behavior changed over time. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

When comparing a policy, ask the insurer about its history of rate increases and the circumstances under which premiums can change.

Don't budget only for today's premium

If you are considering coverage for decades, understand how future premium changes could affect your retirement or household budget.

Long-term care insurance and Medicaid

Medicaid can cover certain long-term care services for people who meet the program's eligibility rules. Those rules can depend on income, assets, medical needs and state-specific requirements.

Long-term care insurance is one possible way people may plan for care costs before Medicaid eligibility becomes relevant.

Because Medicaid rules vary by state and can involve significant financial and legal considerations, avoid assuming that a particular asset or insurance policy will affect eligibility in a specific way without checking the applicable rules.

Types of long-term care coverage

Traditional long-term care insurance

Traditional LTC insurance is primarily designed to provide long-term care benefits when the policy's eligibility requirements are met.

Hybrid life insurance policies

Some life insurance policies can include long-term care benefits or offer long-term care features as an additional option. These products combine different forms of insurance protection and can have substantially different costs and rules.

Employer or group coverage

Some employers and associations offer group long-term care coverage. Group policies can have different underwriting and benefit structures from individual policies. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

What to consider before buying long-term care insurance

There is no universal age or financial situation at which everyone should purchase long-term care insurance. The decision can depend on your health, income, assets, retirement plans, family situation and preferences about where you would want to receive care.

NAIC specifically recommends considering your financial situation, health, retirement goals and desired living arrangements when evaluating long-term care coverage. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}

1

Estimate potential care costs

Look at the cost of care where you expect to live rather than relying on a national average.

2

Review your assets

Consider retirement accounts, savings, investments and other resources that could potentially be used for care.

3

Consider your family situation

Think about whether relatives could realistically provide care and how that could affect both the caregiver and the household.

4

Decide where you would prefer care

Some people prefer home care, while others may eventually need assisted living or nursing facility care.

5

Compare actual policy language

Compare benefit triggers, exclusions, elimination periods, inflation protection, benefit limits and premiums.

Long-term care insurance shopping checklist

Before purchasing a policy, ask the insurer or agent these questions:

  1. What types of care and facilities are covered?
  2. What are the daily or monthly benefit limits?
  3. What is the maximum benefit period or benefit pool?
  4. What triggers eligibility for benefits?
  5. How long is the elimination period?
  6. How is the elimination period counted?
  7. Does the policy include inflation protection?
  8. How could premiums change in the future?
  9. What exclusions or limitations apply?
  10. What happens if I stop paying premiums?
  11. Is the insurance company licensed in my state?
  12. What is the insurer's history of rate increases?

Common long-term care insurance mistakes

  • Assuming Medicare pays for all long-term care. Medicare generally does not cover ongoing custodial long-term care.
  • Comparing policies only by premium. Benefit limits and policy definitions can be just as important.
  • Ignoring inflation. Care costs can be substantially different decades after a policy is purchased.
  • Not understanding the elimination period. The way the waiting period is calculated can affect your initial out-of-pocket costs.
  • Assuming every policy covers home care the same way. Coverage and limits vary by contract.
  • Ignoring premium increase history. Future premiums can matter significantly when planning retirement expenses.
  • Not checking state licensing. Verify that the insurer and agent are authorized to sell the coverage where you live.

Long-term care insurance FAQs

What is long-term care insurance?

Long-term care insurance is designed to help pay for qualifying extended care when a person needs ongoing assistance because of illness, disability, cognitive impairment or another qualifying condition.

Does Medicare cover long-term care?

Medicare generally does not cover long-term custodial care. It can cover certain skilled nursing and other services under specific circumstances, but that is different from ongoing long-term care.

What does long-term care insurance cover?

Depending on the policy, coverage may include qualifying home care, assisted living, adult day care, nursing facility care and other covered long-term care services.

What is an elimination period?

It is the period that must be satisfied before benefits begin. Policies differ in how they define and count the elimination period.

Why is inflation protection important?

Inflation protection can increase benefits over time, helping the policy maintain more purchasing power if long-term care costs rise.

Can Medicaid pay for long-term care?

Medicaid can cover certain long-term care services for people who meet applicable federal and state eligibility requirements. Rules vary by state.

Can long-term care insurance premiums increase?

Premium increases can occur with long-term care insurance. Ask about the insurer's rate-increase history and the policy's provisions before purchasing coverage.

Does long-term care insurance cover home care?

Many policies can provide benefits for qualifying home care, but coverage, limits and eligibility requirements vary by policy.
