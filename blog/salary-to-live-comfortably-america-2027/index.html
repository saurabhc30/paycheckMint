How Much Salary Do You Need to Live Comfortably in America in 2027?

Real 2026 benchmarks by state, city and household type, plus a clear estimate of what to aim for in 2027.

By the PaycheckMint Team · Updated November 2026 · 9 min read

$200K+Needed by a family of four in 40 states
$87,460Median U.S. household income in 2025 (Census)
~3%Assumed cost growth used for our 2027 estimate

Earning "enough" used to mean covering rent, groceries and a car payment. Today most people also want savings, a vacation and room for surprises. That is what living comfortably means, and it is a much higher bar than getting by.

Quick answer A single adult needs roughly $80,000 to $130,000 a year before taxes to live comfortably, depending on the state. A family of four with two working adults needs about $190,000 to $330,000. If costs rise about 3% by 2027, that becomes around $82,000–$133,000 for singles and $193,000–$340,000 for families (our estimate, not a published figure).

What Does "Living Comfortably" Actually Mean?

There is no official definition, which is why estimates vary. The most cited recent study, by SmartAsset, starts with the MIT Living Wage Calculator, which prices housing, food, transportation, health care and childcare by location. It then applies the 50/30/20 rule: 50% of after-tax income for needs, 30% for wants, 20% for savings and debt repayment.

The result is the pre-tax income needed to cover essentials and have room for fun and savings. That differs from a living wage (the minimum to cover needs) and from the poverty line. Keep that in mind when comparing your pay with any headline number.

The Reality Check: Median Income in America

The Census Bureau reports real median household income of $87,460 in 2025, up 2.6% from 2024 and the highest on record since 1967. After taxes and credits, median post-tax household income was $76,060.

A median household often has more than one earner, and the comfort benchmarks below exceed that median for singles in most states. That gap explains why many people with decent paychecks still feel stretched. Household income only recently topped its 2019 level after years of rising prices.

Comfortable Salary Benchmarks for 2026–2027

Headline findings from SmartAsset's 2026 study (MIT data updated February 2026):

  • Single adult: at least $80,000 in every state, above $100,000 in nearly half.
  • Family of four (two earners, two kids): at least $200,000 in 40 states.
  • Most expensive: Massachusetts (family of four: $329,555) and Hawaii (single adult: $129,002).
  • Least expensive: Mississippi (family of four: $187,533) and West Virginia.
  • Biggest jump: Montana, up 8.6% in a year for a single adult.
  • Costs fell in Tennessee, Maryland, Louisiana, North Carolina, Mississippi and Texas.

Salary to live comfortably as a family of four

Kids change the math, mostly through childcare and health coverage. With two working parents, the threshold ranges from about $187,533 in Mississippi to $329,555 in Massachusetts. Maryland sits near $257,837. For most of the country, raising two kids comfortably takes $200,000 or more in combined household income.

What Changes in 2027?

No 2027 numbers exist yet, because studies lag by a year. These forces will shape them:

  • Housing: rent and mortgages are the biggest "needs" item.
  • Inflation: even 2–3% a year raises the threshold by thousands of dollars.
  • Wage growth: if pay outpaces prices, as in 2025 when real median income grew 2.6%, the gap narrows.
  • Regional shifts: costs fell in some states and spiked in others, so national averages hide a lot.
Planning tip: take the 2026 figure for your state, add 3%, then add a buffer if you rent, plan to have children or may move to a pricier city.

If essentials already eat more than half your income, your salary is likely under the comfort line for your area, or your fixed costs are too high.

How to Reach Your Comfortable Salary

  1. Calculate your own number.

    Total your essential monthly costs, double it to estimate after-tax income needed, then gross it up for taxes. Use your real rent.

  2. Negotiate and upskill.

    Research pay for your role in your metro area. In-demand skills and certifications usually beat waiting for annual raises.

  3. Cut your biggest fixed cost.

    Housing is usually the largest. Roommates or a cheaper neighborhood can save more than dozens of small cuts.

  4. Consider geographic arbitrage.

    Remote workers can earn a big-city salary in a low-cost state, potentially freeing about $20,000 a year in needs.

  5. Add income streams.

    Side work, freelancing or a second earner can close the gap faster than one promotion.

  6. Automate savings first.

    Treat the 20% like a bill. Even 5–10% builds the emergency fund that makes life feel comfortable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is $100,000 a good salary in 2027?

It is solid and near the comfort line for a single adult in many states, but short for families. In New York City or the Bay Area, a single adult may need $150,000 or more.

What salary is considered middle class?

Median household income was $87,460 in 2025. Middle class depends on household size and location, and comfort benchmarks now sit above the median.

Can a single person live comfortably on $60,000?

You can live on it, especially in lower-cost areas, but it is below most 2026 comfort benchmarks, which start near $80,000 even in the cheapest states.

Which state is cheapest to live comfortably in?

Mississippi and West Virginia ranked least expensive in the 2026 study. Massachusetts and Hawaii ranked most expensive.

Do these figures include taxes?

They are pre-tax salaries, sized to leave enough after taxes to follow the 50/30/20 rule.

Final Thoughts

There is no single magic number. A comfortable 2027 salary could be $85,000 in parts of the South and Midwest, or well over $150,000 in expensive coastal cities. Use your state and city benchmark, add a margin for inflation, and compare it with your real budget. Knowing the target is the first step toward closing the gap.
