Medicare Part B Premium Hike 2027: How Much of Your COLA Is Really Left?

By PaycheckMint · Published November 21, 2026 · 11 min read

Quick answer: The standard Medicare Part B premium rises $6.50 to $209.40 a month on January 1, 2027 (a 3.2% increase), and the Part B deductible rises $9 to $292. Social Security's 3.6% COLA is slightly bigger, so the average retired worker still keeps about $68.50 of a roughly $75 monthly raise. Higher earners can pay far more through IRMAA, with total monthly premiums as high as $711.80.

Every November the same headline lands on retirees: the Medicare Part B premium is going up. This year the number is $6.50 a month, which sounds small until you remember where the money comes from. For most people with Medicare, the premium is taken out of the Social Security check before a single dollar reaches the bank account. That means the Medicare Part B premium hike for 2027 is really a question about your raise: how much of it do you actually get to spend?

The short answer is more than last year, and a lot more than the scary headlines suggest, unless your income puts you in an IRMAA bracket, where the picture changes quickly. Below you will find the official 2027 numbers from the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS), a calculator to test your own benefit, the full income-based premium tables, and seven practical moves to soften the hit before the new year.

Is this Medicare Part B premium hike big or small?

Compare it with last year. The 2026 premium jumped $17.90, or 9.7%, while the 2026 COLA was only 2.8%. That meant Part B grew more than three times faster than benefits. For 2027, CMS set the increase at 3.2%, and the COLA is 3.6%. This is the first time since 2023 that the premium percentage rise is smaller than the Social Security raise. CMS says the 2027 increase is mainly due to projected increases in health care spending.

Smaller does not mean free. Over a full year, the standard premium goes from $2,434.80 to $2,512.80, which is $78 more out of pocket. If you also pay for Part D, a Medigap or Medicare Advantage plan, or you land in an IRMAA bracket, the total bill moves in ways the headline number does not show. If you want to see how gross benefits turn into spendable income, start with gross pay vs. net pay.

Most people never see a Part A premium. CMS notes that approximately 99% of beneficiaries have at least 40 quarters of Medicare-covered work and pay nothing for Part A. For people who keep Part B only for immunosuppressive drug coverage after a kidney transplant, the 2027 standard premium is $125.70.

How much of your 3.6% COLA is left after Part B?

The Social Security Administration announced a 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment for 2027, which lifts the average retired worker's benefit by about $75 a month, from $2,095 to $2,170. Subtract the $6.50 Part B increase and the average retiree is ahead by about $68.50 a month, or $822 a year. Put another way, Part B takes less than 9 cents of every new dollar for that average retiree.

Part B premium history: 2025 to 2027

Looking at three years side by side shows why 2027 feels like a break in the pattern.

Year Standard Part B premium Increase Part B deductible Social Security COLA
2025 $185.00 - $257 2.5%
2026 $202.90 +$17.90 (9.7%) $283 2.8%
2027 $209.40 +$6.50 (3.2%) $292 3.6%

Over two years the standard premium has risen by $24.40 a month, which is about $293 a year. That is the real reason Medicare costs feel heavy even in a year with a modest increase: the base keeps stepping up.

The IRMAA surprise: 2027 Medicare premiums by income

The $209.40 figure is the standard premium, and it only applies if your modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) is at or below $112,000 (single) or $224,000 (married filing jointly). Above that, an income-related monthly adjustment amount, known as IRMAA, is added on top. CMS says IRMAA affects roughly 9% of people with Part B.

Married people who lived with their spouse at any time during the year but file separate returns face tighter brackets: $209.40 at $112,000 or less, $670.00 above $112,000 and under $388,000, and $711.80 at $388,000 or more. The matching Part D adjustments are $88.30 and $96.40.

Why IRMAA feels like a cliff

IRMAA works in steps, not slopes. Cross a threshold by a single dollar and the whole tier applies. Being just over $112,000 as a single filer adds $83.70 to Part B and $15.40 to Part D, which is $99.10 a month, or $1,189.20 a year. Social Security generally looks at your tax return from two years earlier to set the bracket, so a large one-time income event, such as selling a home or taking a big retirement withdrawal, can raise your Medicare bill two years later.

The CMS tables above are the official 2027 amounts. Your own bracket is set by Social Security, so watch for your notice in the mail and read it closely.

7 moves to soften the Medicare Part B hike

You cannot change the standard premium, but you can control a surprising amount of what you pay around it. Work through these before January 1.

1. Check your IRMAA notice and challenge it if your income dropped

If your bracket was based on income from a year when you were still working and your income has since fallen, you may be able to ask Social Security to use a more recent year. Qualifying events include retirement or reduced work hours, marriage, divorce, the death of a spouse, and loss of income-producing property or a pension.

What to do: Read the IRMAA letter, then ask Social Security about the life-changing event form (SSA-44) and bring documents that show the change in income.

2. Use open enrollment before it closes

Medicare's annual open enrollment period runs October 15 to December 7. That is your window to switch Medicare Advantage or Part D plans for 2027. You generally still pay the Part B premium on a Medicare Advantage plan, but plan premiums, drug coverage and out-of-pocket maximums vary, and some plans offer a Part B premium reduction.

What to do: Compare your current plan's 2027 costs with at least two alternatives using Medicare's plan finder, and check that your doctors and drugs are still covered.

3. See whether a Medicare Savings Program can pay your premium

Medicare Savings Programs are run by states and can cover Part B premiums for people with limited income and resources. Some also help with deductibles and coinsurance. Limits differ by state and change each year.

What to do: Contact your state Medicaid office or your local State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) for free counseling, and ask about Extra Help for Part D costs at the same time.

4. Budget with your net deposit, not your gross benefit

The number you plan around should be the amount that lands in your account, after Part B and any other deductions. When the January check arrives, the raise will already have the new premium subtracted. Build next year's plan around that figure so the $6.50 does not turn into a surprise.

What to do: Rework your monthly plan with our paycheck budget guide and run the numbers in the take-home pay calculator.

5. Watch your income near an IRMAA line

If you are within a few thousand dollars of a bracket, the timing of withdrawals, Roth conversions, capital gains and other taxable income can matter a lot, because crossing the line by one dollar triggers the full surcharge for the year. Because IRMAA uses income from two years earlier, decisions you make in 2026 can affect 2028 premiums.

What to do: Before a big income event, ask a tax professional to estimate your MAGI and how close it comes to the next threshold. Our tax guides cover the basics.

6. Ask whether your premiums are tax deductible

Medicare premiums can count as medical expenses. If you itemize deductions and your total medical costs clear the IRS threshold, or if you are self-employed, part of what you pay may reduce your taxes. Many retirees take the standard deduction, so this will not help everyone.

What to do: Keep your premium records, including Part B, Part D and supplemental plans, and review them with a tax preparer at filing time.

7. Build a cushion for the deductibles

The premium is only part of the cost. A single hospital admission can start with a $1,776 Part A deductible in 2027, and Part B requires $292 before it starts sharing outpatient costs. Without supplemental coverage, 20% coinsurance can add up quickly.

What to do: Set aside money for medical costs in a dedicated account. Our guide to building an emergency fund walks through how to start small.

Who feels the 2027 Part B increase the most?

Retirees with lower benefits. A flat $6.50 takes a bigger bite out of a $1,000 check than a $3,000 one, as the table above shows.

Retirees near an IRMAA line. The standard hike is small compared with a bracket jump of $83.70 or more per month.

People who pay for several parts of Medicare. Part B, Part D, Medigap or Medicare Advantage premiums each move on their own schedule, so a modest Part B change can still sit on top of larger increases elsewhere.

New enrollees. If you turn 65 in 2027, the first premium you see will be the new one, and late enrollment penalties can apply if you skip Part B without other qualifying coverage.

Frequently asked questions

How much is Medicare Part B in 2027?

The standard monthly Medicare Part B premium is $209.40 in 2027, up $6.50 from $202.90 in 2026. The annual Part B deductible is $292, up $9 from $283. Higher-income enrollees pay more through income-related monthly adjustment amounts (IRMAA), up to a total of $711.80 per month.

Why is the Medicare Part B premium going up in 2027?

CMS says the increase in the 2027 Part B premium and deductible is mainly due to projected increases in health care spending. The 3.2% rise is much smaller than the 9.7% increase in 2026.

When does the new Part B premium start?

The new Medicare Part B premium, deductible and cost-sharing amounts take effect on January 1, 2027. If your premium is deducted from Social Security, the higher amount comes out of your January 2027 benefit.

Will the Part B increase eat my entire Social Security raise?

No for most people. The 2027 Social Security COLA is 3.6%, which is about $75 per month for the average retired worker, while the standard Part B increase is $6.50 per month. That leaves roughly $68.50 more per month before other costs such as Part D, supplemental insurance or taxes.

What is the Medicare Part B deductible in 2027?

The Part B deductible is $292 in 2027, up $9 from $283 in 2026. After you meet it, Part B generally covers 80% of approved amounts for most outpatient services and you pay 20% coinsurance unless you have supplemental coverage.

What is IRMAA and who pays it in 2027?

IRMAA is the income-related monthly adjustment amount, an extra charge on Part B and Part D premiums for higher earners. In 2027 it applies when modified adjusted gross income is above $112,000 for individuals or $224,000 for joint filers. CMS says it affects roughly 9% of people with Part B.

Can I avoid the higher Part B premium?

Most people cannot avoid the standard premium. You may be able to lower an IRMAA charge if your income dropped because of a life-changing event such as retirement, and people with limited income and assets may qualify for a Medicare Savings Program that helps pay Part B premiums. Rules and limits vary, so confirm with Social Security or your state program.

Does Medicare Advantage change what I pay for Part B?

You generally still pay the Part B premium if you are in a Medicare Advantage plan, plus any premium the plan charges. Some plans offer a Part B premium reduction. Compare plans during open enrollment, which runs October 15 to December 7.
